Project Annapurna

A working name for the prototype below — an AI-personalized, biomarker-aware Indian meal planner that hands you the recipe, not just the food name.

Problem
People get generic diet advice that ignores their actual biomarkers and doesn't reflect how India eats.
Objective
Analyze biomarkers, dietary preference, and health goals to generate personalized weekly or monthly Indian meal plans — with a full recipe for every meal — grounded in current nutritional science.
This plan
Scopes a self-contained prototype (no real backend, no live model calls) that demonstrates the full experience convincingly.
Audience & scope

What this prototype is for

Built as a personal proof-of-concept — to prove the end-to-end idea works and feels right, not as a clinically validated product.

In scope

  • Pick a sample persona or enter your own biomarkers, preferences, and goals
  • Weekly (7-day) and monthly (4-week) plan modes
  • Rule-based personalization presented as an AI analysis
  • Full recipe for every meal — ingredients, steps, nutrition, prep time
  • Indian regional coverage: North, South, East, West
  • Vegetarian, vegan, eggetarian, non-vegetarian, Jain diets

Out of scope

  • Real lab report parsing or wearable/device integration
  • Live LLM or nutrition-API calls
  • Clinical validation of any recommendation
  • Accounts, payments, long-term progress tracking
  • Dietitian-reviewed, exhaustive recipe database
Research · what exists today

The landscape

Biomarker-first personalized nutrition is established globally but food-logging still dominates in India. That gap is where this prototype sits.

ZOE — microbiome + blood biomarkers

At-home test analyzes gut microbiome, blood sugar, and blood fat response; app scores meals daily against that personal profile.

play.google.com — ZOE

InsideTracker — ~120 biomarkers

Combines blood, DNA, and fitness-tracker data; maps nutrient intake directly onto blood biomarker movement over time.

nutritioninsight.com — InsideTracker

Nutrisense — CGM-driven

Continuous glucose monitoring paired with dietitian coaching, bundled with InsideTracker's blood panel for a combined metabolic view.

nutrisense.io — CGM bundle

HealthifyMe — India's leader

10,000+ Indian foods database; "Ria" AI coach personalizes from food logs, exercise, sleep — but starts from logging, not biomarkers.

healthifyme.com

Fitelo — AI + human coach

AI-enabled analysis of habits and medical history, paired with a dietitian/doctor/trainer team; 20,000+ clients across 15+ countries.

indialt.in — Fitelo

NutriScan — Indian-food-native tracking

Built specifically around Indian food culture for meal logging and nutrition insight, rather than biomarker-to-plan generation.

nutriscan.app

The whitespace: ZOE and InsideTracker start from biomarkers but aren't Indian-food-aware. HealthifyMe, Fitelo, and NutriScan know Indian food deeply but start from logging, not biomarkers — and none hand you a full, cook-ready recipe as the output. A biomarker-first flow that ends in an actual Indian recipe is the gap this prototype fills.

Research · the science backbone

Grounding the logic in real nutrition science

Two Indian data sources anchor the rule engine so the plan isn't just plausible-looking, it's traceable to actual guidance.

ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines / My Plate

Government baseline: 8 food groups per plate; cereals ≤45% of energy, pulses/eggs/flesh foods ~14–15%, total fat ≤30%, nuts/oilseeds/dairy 8–10%. Used as the default balance before any biomarker adjustment.

nin.res.in — My Plate 2024

Glycemic index of Indian foods

200+ Indian foods indexed for GI/GL — e.g. besan roti (GI 28) vs. wheat roti (GI 60) vs. maize roti (GI 63) — the lever for blood-sugar-flagged personas.

glycemicindia.com
Product

How Annapurna works, screen by screen

Start

Choose "Try a sample profile" or "Enter my own info." Either path leads to the same engine.

Profile

Sample: pick from six personas spanning regions, diets, and biomarker flags (below). Manual: a short form for biomarkers (with a normal-range hint next to each field), dietary preference, region preference, and health goal — plus the weekly/monthly toggle.

"Analyzing"

A brief scripted sequence narrates the read-out — "Reading HbA1c… flagging insulin sensitivity… matching low-GI staples" — before the plan renders. It's the rule engine below, dramatized.

Plan grid

Days × meals (breakfast, mid-morning, lunch, snack, dinner). Monthly mode adds a week selector. A rationale strip at the top ties the whole plan back to the inputs that shaped it.

Recipe detail

Click any meal to open its full recipe: ingredients in Indian units, method, nutrition estimate, prep time, and the one-line reason it was chosen for this profile.

Data approach

The personalization logic, made honest

No live model call. Inputs — sample or manual — resolve to a small set of flags, and flags filter a curated, tagged recipe pool. It's deterministic, but the on-screen framing treats it as an analysis, since that's the experience being prototyped.

glucose / HbA1c ↑

Prioritize low-GI grains (millets, besan/chana roti), pulses-forward meals, no added sugar.

LDL / triglycerides ↑

Reduce ghee-fried preparations, raise fiber and nuts (moderated), favor mustard-oil / steamed regional options.

vitamin D / B12 / iron ↓

Weight toward eggs (if non-veg/eggetarian), mushrooms, ragi, jaggery, and iron-rich leafy greens.

thyroid (TSH) flag

Iodine-aware substitutions; note moderation on raw cruciferous vegetables.

goal: weight loss

Cap portion/calorie targets; raise protein-to-carb ratio within the diet type.

goal: muscle gain

Raise protein sources — paneer, dals, egg, chicken — within the diet type and region.

diet + region

Filters the recipe pool (e.g. Jain excludes onion/garlic/root vegetables) and weights it toward the chosen region without losing variety.

~20–24 tagged recipes across the four regions and five diet types is enough headroom for a non-repeating week and a rotating four-week month.

Tech approach

How it gets built

A single self-contained page. No framework — the interactions are input → filter a local dataset → render a grid, which vanilla HTML/CSS/JS handles cleanly. The recipe and rule dataset lives as an embedded JS object. localStorage remembers the last profile used between visits, as a convenience only — nothing is shared across viewers or stored server-side, so no live backend is needed for this prototype.

Before building

Open questions & risks

  • Not medical advice. The prototype should say so on-screen — the logic is illustrative, not clinically validated.
  • Recipe breadth is deliberately limited. A real product needs a much larger, dietitian-reviewed database; this prototype trades breadth for a working demo.
  • Monthly view will rotate, not hand-author, 30 days. A small set of weekly templates repeats with light variation across four weeks — confirm that's an acceptable shortcut for a prototype.
